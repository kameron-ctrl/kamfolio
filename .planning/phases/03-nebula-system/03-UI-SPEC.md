## Color

### Dominant Atmosphere (60%) — Deep Space Field

| Token | CSS Variable | Rendered Value | Usage |
|-------|-------------|----------------|-------|
| Space base | `--color-space` | `oklch(4% 0 0)` ≈ `#0a0a0a` | Canvas background; Layer 1 deep space gradient anchor |
| Near-void | `--color-void` | `oklch(8% 0.01 260)` ≈ `#0d0d1a` | Layer 1 gradient midpoint; introduces cool blue-violet cast |

Source: `src/styles/tokens.css` — already declared in Phase 1.

**Layer 1 character (deep space gradient):** Radial gradient centered slightly above-center viewport. Core: `--color-void` (#0d0d1a). Edge: `--color-space` (#0a0a0a). Result: a faint cool-blue atmospheric center surrounded by true darkness. Not a flat fill — the gradient must be perceptible.

### Secondary Ambient (30%) — Star Field + Far Nebula

| Layer | Color | Opacity Range |
|-------|-------|---------------|
| Layer 2 — stars | `#c8d8ff` (pale blue-white) | 0.6–1.0 per particle via alpha |
| Layer 3 — far nebula clusters | `#7b4fa6` purple, `#2ec4b6` teal, `#ff6b6b` coral | 0.15–0.25 at layer level |
| Layer 4 — mid volumetric fog | Same 3 cluster colors at lower saturation | 0.08–0.15 at layer level |

### Accent / Foreground (10%) — Near Layers

| Layer | Color | Opacity Range | Visual Character |
|-------|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| Layer 5 — near bokeh | `#7b4fa6`, `#2ec4b6`, `#ff6b6b` | 0.20–0.35 | Large soft discs drifting across the moon face |
| Layer 6 — foreground dust | `#d0e0ff` (cool white) | 0.06–0.12 | Tiny grain; barely visible micro-texture |

### Three Nebula Cluster Colors — Locked

Source: CONTEXT.md D-11, `src/styles/tokens.css` — already declared.

| Name | Hex | CSS Variable | AdditiveBlending visual result |
|------|-----|-------------|-------------------------------|
| Nebula purple | `#7b4fa6` | `--color-nebula-purple` | Blooms to lavender-white at peak opacity overlap |
| Nebula teal | `#2ec4b6` | `--color-nebula-teal` | Blooms to cyan-white at peak opacity overlap |
| Nebula coral | `#ff6b6b` | `--color-nebula-coral` | Blooms to warm pink-white at peak opacity overlap |

**AdditiveBlending effect contract:** Because all particle layers use `THREE.AdditiveBlending`, particles do not occlude each other — overlapping particles sum their luminance. At single-particle opacity of 0.2, a cluster center where 10+ particles overlap reads as near-white with a color tint. Cluster edges bleed into thin color halos. This bloom-like appearance is intentional and must not be suppressed by reducing per-particle opacity so much that the additive accumulation disappears. Target: cluster center reads as at least 60% luminance when viewed against the dark background.

### Color Temperature Rule

The overall scene must read as **cold** (blue-dominant). Purple and teal clusters occupy the majority of screen area. Coral is a single warm-accent cluster for contrast. Coral must not dominate. Canonical spatial placement: coral cluster lower-right quadrant, offset from center — never centered with the moon.

## Layer Visual Character

This section is the primary design contract for this phase. Each layer has a distinct visual character the executor must match. "Character" describes what a still frame should look like and what the parallax motion should feel like.

### Layer 1 — Deep Space Gradient

- **What it looks like:** A nearly-black background with a subtle cool-blue haze that is denser toward the center of the viewport and fades to pure black at the edges. Perceptible but not distracting. A viewer who has not seen the before-state should sense the atmosphere is intentional, not a default fill.
- **Implementation contract:** CSS `radial-gradient` on the canvas wrapper div. Value: `radial-gradient(ellipse at 50% 40%, var(--color-void) 0%, var(--color-space) 70%)`. This approach requires setting `gl={{ alpha: true }}` on the Canvas and removing `<color attach="background">` from `MoonScene.tsx` (see RESEARCH.md Pitfall 6 for the exact two-file change required).
- **Parallax:** 0.0× (fixed — does not move).
- **Breathe:** Does not participate in breathe animation.

### Layer 2 — Distant Stars

- **What it looks like:** 2000–3000 tiny white-blue points scattered across the full viewport. Stars are not uniform in brightness — simulate magnitude variation with per-particle size variation (0.008–0.025 world units) and opacity variation (0.4–1.0 via vertex colors or per-geometry alpha). The field must feel genuinely sparse; stars must not read as a noise texture. Individual stars must be distinguishable.
- **Sprite character:** Sharp disc, 16×16 canvas texture, very hard edge (gradient blur stop at 0.1). No visible glow halo.
- **Color:** `#c8d8ff` on `THREE.PointsMaterial`. Stars should be sparse enough that overlap is rare despite AdditiveBlending.
- **Parallax:** 0.05×. Nearly imperceptible motion — stars are effectively fixed. Slight drift visible only at fast scroll or peak breathe.
- **Breathe:** Participates at the same multiplier as all layers. The gentle drift during peak velocity confirms the scene is live.

### Layer 3 — Far Nebula Clusters

- **What it looks like:** Three distinct cloud masses. Each cloud is a roughly oval or irregular soft blob approximately 2–3 world units in diameter. Clouds have visible density variation — denser at center, trailing off at edges with a color halo. Clusters are far enough back that individual particles are not distinguishable; the mass reads as a luminous cloud, not as separate dots.
- **Sprite character:** Soft gaussian blob, 32×32 canvas texture, radial gradient from white center to transparent edge (blur stop at 0.5). Particle size: 0.08–0.15 world units.
- **Cluster placement (world space, all at z = −1.5):**
  - Purple cluster center: approx (−2.5, 1.5). Upper-left, behind and above the moon.
  - Teal cluster center: approx (2.0, 1.0). Upper-right, partially overlapping the moon's lit hemisphere edge.
  - Coral cluster center: approx (2.5, −1.5). Lower-right, below the moon's equator. Coral must not obscure the moon's dark face.
- **Layer opacity:** 0.15–0.20 per `PointsMaterial`. Clusters clearly visible against black but must not overpower the moon.
- **Parallax:** 0.15×. Clouds drift meaningfully when scrolling — a visitor watching the clouds notices movement.
- **Breathe:** Clusters visibly inhale (spread outward, scale up ~1.15–1.2×) during fast scroll.

### Layer 4 — Mid Volumetric Fog

- **What it looks like:** A semi-transparent veil that softens the depth boundary between the star field and the near layers. Not a distinct cloud — more like scattered fog or thin ionized gas. Individual particles are invisible; the effect is a slight luminous haze making the far-to-near transition feel gradual rather than abrupt. Must read as atmospheric volume, not as a particle system.
- **Sprite character:** Same soft gaussian blob as Layer 3, 32×32. Particles are larger (0.12–0.25 world units) but at very low opacity.
- **Color:** Same 3 cluster colors at much reduced opacity. The fog should be barely tinted, not vividly colored. Per-layer `PointsMaterial` opacity: 0.06–0.10.
- **Coverage:** Wide distribution across the full viewport — not confined to cluster positions. Particles distributed across ±6 X, ±4 Y world units.
- **Parallax:** 0.3×. Fog drifts noticeably. The middle speed between slow stars (0.05×) and near bokeh (0.6×) creates the perceived depth midpoint.
- **Breathe:** Participates. Fog spreading during velocity peak reinforces the nebula as a single breathing organism.

### Layer 5 — Near Bokeh

- **What it looks like:** Large, soft defocus discs — classic out-of-focus light bokeh from a camera pointed at a bright scene. 5–15 discs visible at any moment, ranging from 0.3 to 0.8 world units in diameter. Discs drift slowly across the moon's face. When a disc passes over the bright lunar surface, AdditiveBlending creates a subtle glow flare. This layer is what makes the scene feel like viewing the moon through a light atmosphere rather than in hard vacuum.
- **Sprite character:** Large bokeh disc, 64×64 canvas texture, very soft edge (blur stop at 0.3), subtle ring suggestion at the disc edge (classic lens bokeh character). Center slightly brighter than mid-radius.
- **Color:** All three cluster colors — approximately 40% purple, 40% teal, 20% coral by particle count. Per-layer opacity: 0.20–0.30.
- **Particle count:** 80–120 particles total (not 800 — large size compensates for low count). On mobile: 30–50.
- **Parallax:** 0.6×. Clearly and immediately visible parallax motion. Near bokeh discs must obviously move relative to the moon when scrolling.
- **Breathe:** This layer shows the most dramatic breathe behavior. At peak velocity the discs spread across the viewport; as velocity decays they pull back with a damped-settle feel.
- **renderOrder:** 5 — in front of the moon (moon is renderOrder 4). Bokeh discs appear to float in front of the moon's surface.

### Layer 6 — Foreground Dust Motes

- **What it looks like:** The closest-to-camera layer. Tiny, barely-visible dust particles at the very near plane. Individual particles are visible as faint specks. Their fast parallax (0.8×) makes them feel physically close — they visibly drift across the scene even during slow scrolling. Adds micro-texture and reinforces physical depth. Dust must not clutter the scene; it exists at the periphery of perception.
- **Sprite character:** Tiny grain texture, 8×8 canvas texture, sharp but slightly soft-edged (blur stop at 0.2). Particle size: 0.015–0.04 world units.
- **Color:** `#d0e0ff` (cool near-white). No cluster color — dust is colorless. Per-layer opacity: 0.06–0.10. Individual motes must be barely visible, not distracting.
- **Particle count:** 300–500 scattered across a wide near-plane. On mobile: 100–150.
- **Parallax:** 0.8×. Fast and obvious. The contrast between dust at 0.8× and stars at 0.05× is the widest parallax gap in the stack and provides the strongest depth cue.
- **Breathe:** Participates. Fast scroll dramatically sweeps the dust plane — like looking through a porthole into a dusty atmosphere during turbulence.
- **renderOrder:** 6 — closest to viewer.

## Depth Illusion Quality Standard

The parallax stack must create convincing spatial depth. The quality bar is: a visitor who scrolls through the scene must feel they are moving through three-dimensional space, not watching a 2D layer stack with different speeds. Four depth cues must operate simultaneously:

1. **Parallax speed differential:** Stars (0.05×) feel infinitely far. Dust (0.8×) feels close enough to touch. All intermediate layers fill the distance spectrum continuously.
2. **Size perspective:** Near bokeh discs are large and blur-soft. Far nebula clusters are hazy masses. Stars are pinpoints. Size correlates with depth — nothing at far depth appears large and sharp.
3. **Opacity atmosphere:** Distant layers have lower opacity. The scene feels like looking through partial atmosphere, not like painted layers in Photoshop.
4. **Color temperature gradient:** Cool, desaturated at far distances. Warm coral accent only in mid and near layers. Deep space is almost colorless.

Failure modes to reject during QA:
- Layers visible as a "stack of planes" rather than a continuous volume.
- Stars visibly moving at the same rate as nebula clusters.
- Moon appearing to float on top of a 2D background rather than embedded inside a 3D nebula.
- Bokeh discs snapping rather than drifting through the moon plane.

## Velocity Breathe — Animation Feel Contract

The breathe effect must feel **cinematic, not mechanical.** The distinction:

- **Mechanical breathe (reject):** Layer scale pulses proportionally to scroll speed like a meter needle. Visitor is aware of the animation as an animation.
- **Cinematic breathe (required):** At fast scroll, the nebula reacts to disturbance — clouds spread, fog disperses, dust swirls. When scroll stops, layers drift back to rest with a natural damped-settle feel (even though technically it is linear decay from the store's lerp). The visitor feels the nebula is alive.

Breathe parameters:
- **Peak scale multiplier:** 1.25× at maximum `displayVelocity`. (Tune within 1.2–1.3 range during execution — start at 1.25.)
- **Decay feel:** `displayVelocity` from the scroll store is already lerped at factor 0.15, giving roughly 0.6–0.8s settle. No additional smoothing on the nebula side. The settle should feel like the moment after a physical disturbance.
- **Layer differential:** All layers use the same breathe multiplier. Unified response makes all layers feel like one organism reacting to one force.
- **Zero-velocity baseline:** At `displayVelocity` ≈ 0, multiplier = 1.0 exactly. Layers must be completely still when scroll is idle. No idle animation, no ambient drift — that is a future consideration.

## Reduced Motion Contract

Source: REQUIREMENTS.md QUAL-03, `src/styles/tokens.css` `--motion-ok` property.

When `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is active, `--motion-ok` is set to `0` by the existing token. Phase 3 must respect this:

- **Breathe:** Disabled. `displayVelocity` multiplier is clamped to 1.0 regardless of actual velocity.
- **Parallax:** Reduced but not removed. Cut all parallax coefficients to 20% of nominal values (0.05× becomes 0.01×, 0.8× becomes 0.16×). Nebula provides visual atmosphere but does not distract users sensitive to motion.
- **Detection:** Read `window.matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)').matches` once at mount inside `NebulaScene.tsx`. Store as a `useRef` boolean. Do not re-query per frame.
